News Lab reporters reached out by email to the two candidates vying for Pennsylvania’s 15th Congressional District in the U.S. House of Representatives, which includes all of Warren County, as well as Armstrong, Cameron, Centre, Clarion, Clearfield, Clinton, Elk, Forest, Jefferson, McKean, Potter, Snyder, Tioga, Union, and parts of Indiana, Lycoming, and Venango counties.

Glenn Thompson (R) is the incumbent running against Ray Bilger (D). Both candidates responded to our questions.

Answers are published as submitted and have not been edited or changed in any way. Answers have not been edited for length, grammar, spelling or other errors. They have not been fact-checked. Claims candidates make are their own.

## Ray Bilger

<strong>What legislation would you propose or support that would make healthcare more accessible and affordable for constituents in rural PA and, in light of federal funding cuts to Medicaid and the expiration of ACA subsidies, how would you pay for it?</strong>

A universal healthcare model with a private option. Until that is fully vetted and instituted, reinstate funding cuts to the previous programs as a band aid solution. Lives are literally at risk this winter without it. Funding the big solution can be done in various ways. I suggest either legalizing and taxing marijuana, or using the funds gleaned from Venezuelan oil and gold sitting unused in institutions known only to certain persons in the administration.

<strong>What steps would you take to provide relief for rural hospitals like Warren General to ensure they can continue to provide health care services to their communities?</strong>

As mentioned, restore previously cut funding as a band aid solution until a universal healthcare system can be realized. Institute birthing centers to help with prenatal and maternal care. Introduce a bill forcing healthcare insurers to divest from all other healthcare-related industries and provide only healthcare.

<strong>Staffing shortages, including doctors, nurses, and EMS workers, are contributing to the lack of accessibility to healthcare in large swaths of rural Pennsylvania. Please describe any legislative efforts you’d support in addressing healthcare worker shortages in the area you’d serve.</strong>

Immediate relief can be provided by greatly expanding the Conrad 30 program. Expand it to 3000, or whatever number is necessary to fill these hard-to-fill vacancies, and extend the residency requirement to 3 years instead of 2. This will allow time to increase domestic training programs to fill the positions. Also expand the program to include any and all positions which we are unable to fill yet are vital to the recovery and growth of our rural communities.

As I mentioned, the rural Pennsylvania and rural America death spiral is the result of multiple factors over decades. Legislative neglect of these problems is the overall issue. Ignoring hard problems only makes them worse. I know. I grew up in rural PA as a first generation college graduate. I know how hard it is to survive and grow here. That&#39;s why I left PA 40 years ago to achieve my dreams.

Each of the problems must be addressed in order to ensure that we can create rural communities which thrive and survive. The economic, educational, infrastructure, and opportunity aspects of all of these areas must be addressed. If not, we are condemning our rural communities and America itself.

## Glenn Thompson

<strong>What legislation would you propose or support that would make healthcare more accessible and affordable for constituents in rural PA and, in light of federal funding cuts to Medicaid and the expiration of ACA subsidies, how would you pay for it?</strong>

Rural communities deserve access to comprehensive healthcare, especially older residents who tend to be higher consumers of healthcare services. This is why I recently introduced the bipartisan <em>Community Health Center Reforms Enabling Better Access for Seniors and Elders Act </em>(CHC REBASE Act). This legislation will modernize how Medicare pays for Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs), ensuring underserved patients can continue accessing the care they need. I was also pleased to introduce the bipartisan <em>Sustaining Rural Healthcare Act</em>, which will expand access to care and assist hospitals which need financial assistance by creating an “In Character” Critical Access Hospital (CAH) designation. Increased flexibility for at-risk rural hospitals can help ensure our communities have robust access to care.

<strong>What steps would you take to provide relief for rural hospitals like Warren General to ensure they can continue to provide health care services to their communities?</strong>

Earlier this year, I worked with Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz to ensure that Warren General Hospital would obtain a Critical Access Hospital designation. This is a vital designation to provide our rural community with access to high-quality healthcare and help ensure long-term operational stability. In addition, we can continue to make healthcare more accessible for our rural communities through telemedicine. This Congress, I introduced the bipartisan <em>Helping Ensure Access to Local Telehealth (HEALTH) Act</em>, which will secure Medicare reimbursement for telehealth services provided by community health centers and rural health clinics providing services to Pennsylvanians, regardless of their geographic location.

<strong>Staffing shortages, including doctors, nurses, and EMS workers, are contributing to the lack of accessibility to healthcare in large swaths of rural Pennsylvania. Please describe any legislative efforts you would support in addressing healthcare worker shortages in the area you’d serve.</strong>

I proudly spent 30 years working in a non-profit healthcare setting, spent the same amount of time as a volunteer EMT and firefighter, and know firsthand the sacrifices of our first responders. This Congress, I introduced the bipartisan <em>Health Care Worker and First Responder Fairness Act</em>, legislation that will exempt healthcare workers and first responders from Social Security’s Retirement Earnings Test (RET) during public health emergencies. This commonsense policy prevents staff shortages, ensuring hospitals have adequate personnel during national emergencies. I also support the <em>Expand the Behavioral Health Workforce Now Act</em>, legislation that will direct HHS to issue new guidance for states on ways to increase education, training, recruitment, and retention of mental health and substance use disorder care providers under Medicaid and Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP), specifically in rural and underserved areas. These recommendations aim to help states grow their mental health workforce to ensure mental health resources are accessible across the nation, including those in our rural communities.
