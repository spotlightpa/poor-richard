HARRISBURG — Democrats say they will maintain control of Pennsylvania’s House of Representatives. <strong></strong>

The party entered the election with a 103-100 advantage. House Democrats’ campaign committee said <a href="https://x.com/PAHDCC/status/2117834759279960118?s=20">on social media</a> Tuesday night that the party had “officially” secured the 102 seats necessary to protect its majority.

As of 11:30 p.m., the Associated Press <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/11/pennsylvania-election-results-2026-elections/#spl-heading-4">had not called</a> dozens of state House races.

Democrats’ current era of state House control began in 2022 after a redistricting cycle produced new political lines that reflected population shifts from GOP-leaning rural areas to suburban and urban areas.

Over the past four years, Democrats have used their slim majority to advance a constitutional amendment codifying reproductive rights, as well as bills that would increase state funding for mass transit and legalize recreational marijuana.

Those measures died in the state Senate. It remains unclear which party will control that chamber next session.
