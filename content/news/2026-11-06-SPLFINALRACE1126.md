HARRISBURG — Democratic challengers have declared victory in three competitive Pennsylvania House races that will help determine the size of the party’s majority during the next legislative session.

Wins in those races would give the party a 13-seat advantage.

In Lancaster County’s 41st House District, Brad Chambers leads Republican state Rep. Brett Miller by 30 votes. That tally includes unchallenged provisional, military, and overseas ballots that were added to the county’s election website Friday afternoon.

Seven provisional ballots were challenged and held from that total, according to a county spokesperson, and will be considered during a Board of Elections hearing next Thursday. There are also 14 outstanding overseas and military ballots that have until 5 p.m. on Tuesday to reach the elections office.

Chambers declared victory Friday afternoon, while Miller has not publicly commented on the outcome. He also did not respond to requests for comment from Spotlight PA. Neither did the House Republican Campaign Committee.

“I’m humbled to officially become your State Representative-elect for Pennsylvania’s 41st House District,” Chambers said on social media. “I want to thank every single voter who allowed me to listen to their stories, who welcomed my perspective, and who will now trust me to be their voice in Harrisburg.”

Tim Hayes and Margaret Burke, the Democratic candidates in Buck County’s <a href="https://www.buckscountyherald.com/news/election/with-180-vote-lead-democrat-tim-hayes-declares-victory-in-143rd-district/article_cf401dbb-f8f5-402f-8782-d66c9a34797c.html">143rd</a> and Montgomery County’s <a href="https://www.facebook.com/BurkeforPAStateRep/posts/pfbid0Fj8DF4eXFXCb6vpdoJnd8ajexSzxzwksJ2iUKsKBTn6Kh6j3xdeuF7PWTFWVuRSel">147th</a> Districts, respectively, declared victory earlier in the week. The margins in those races were much wider than the 41st’s — over 200 votes in both, per unofficial results Friday afternoon.

In the 143rd District, Bucks County spokesperson Jim O’Malley told Spotlight PA that 220 ballots are still being processed. The county has until a week after the election to consider provisional, military, and overseas ballots.

Megan Alt, Montgomery County communications director, said Friday afternoon that there are “currently up to 230 outstanding ballots that could potentially affect the race” for the 147th District.

Unofficial results show the Republican incumbents in those races — Shelby Labs in the 143rd and Donna Scheuren in the 147th — would need to win nearly every outstanding vote to overtake their Democratic opponents.

They had not conceded as of Friday afternoon, and neither responded to requests for comment from Spotlight PA.

Even before results are certified, the Associated Press declares unofficial winners in races when it determines the trailing candidate has no path to victory. It had not made calls in the three state House races where Democrats declared victory as of Friday afternoon, nor in the 12th House District in Butler County, where Democratic incumbent Brandon Dukes has <a href="https://www.wpxi.com/news/local/pennsylvania-2026-midterm-elections-live-updates-voters-head-polls/M5WSOMHJIBF3TFQQPWMA6M5DCE/?fbclid=IwY2xjawVa4ulleHRuA2FlbQIxMABwZG9mAWJyaWQRMTNsMjFqQ0ZjTGpUOVlnUWVzcnRjBmFwcF9pZBAyMjIwMzkxNzg4MjAwODkyAAEfZ1A7RCVnV3ifhnScXzwFyYS32f3rvBqdR-iiJLrkMHSmpkzTLfvRPyWD1kA_aem_QCCGLbaFPd-G9PhzIe6OLg#:~:text=Brandon%20Dukes%20issued%20a%20statement%2C%20conceding%20the%20race%20for%20PA%20State%20House%20District%2012%3A">conceded</a>.

State law allows for voters to <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2024/06/pennsylvania-election-recounts-explainer-donald-trump-joe-biden/">initiate recounts</a>. Three voters in a precinct can request one based on their belief that fraud or error occurred. Such recounts only affect ballots cast in the voters’ precinct, which is the smallest voting district, usually just a few hundred voters and sometimes no larger than a city block. The fee to request such a recount is $50.

Pennsylvania Democrats are already celebrating this year’s election results. The party flipped the state Senate for the first time in decades, and with gains in the House and the reelection of Gov. Josh Shapiro, the party will hold a <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/11/pennsylvania-democrats-trifecta-agenda-minimum-wage-capitol/">trifecta in Harrisburg</a>.

That power will allow Democrats to pursue a number of policy priorities they say Republicans have stalled, like raising the minimum wage, increasing funding for mass transit, and codifying abortion access.

House Democrats will hold leadership elections on Tuesday, according to a spokesperson.
