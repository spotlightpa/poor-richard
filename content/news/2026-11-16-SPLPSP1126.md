HARRISBURG — Pennsylvania State Police have launched an internal investigation into the use of a slogan — “Hurdle the dead, trample the weak, protect the innocent” — that appeared several times throughout new facilities and stables for its tactical mounted unit, Spotlight PA has learned.

In response to questions sent Friday, State Police spokesperson Logan Brouse said the phrase is not an official slogan of the department.

“All articles that display or reference the slogan have been ordered to be removed, and an internal affairs investigation has been initiated,” Brouse said Monday.

Brouse declined to answer follow-up questions about how long the slogan has been in use, in what scenarios troopers would expect to “hurdle the dead” or “trample the weak,” or when the internal investigation launched.

But Spotlight PA found the slogan has long been associated with the State Police tactical mounted unit.

Rod Witherite, a retired state trooper who now leads the police department in Clearfield Borough, said the phrase has been in use for decades.

“The phrase connected with the mounted unit in the Pennsylvania State Police my entire career,” said Witherite, who served in the department from 1989 to 2013. Older troopers he was assigned with “knew that phrase very well,” Witherite said.

Witherite was not a member of the tactical mounted division, but recalled older troopers explaining the words as an old cavalry slogan that may have originated with Attila the Hun.

The phrase “Hurdle the dead, trample the weak” is well-worn in sports and music.

The metal band Skinless used it as the title of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trample_the_Weak,_Hurdle_the_Dead">their 2006 album</a>, and country singer Ted Nugent used it as the banner for <a href="https://www.tnonline.com/20100622/trample-the-weak-hurdle-the-dead-tour/">his 2010 tour</a>. A review of newspaper archives found <a href="https://www.tennessean.com/story/sports/nfl/titans/2016/07/29/tennessee-titans-locker-room-renovation-trample-weak-hurdle-dead/87762794/?gnt-cfr=1&amp;gca-cat=p&amp;gca-uir=true&amp;gca-epti=z116001e1172xxv116001d--67--b--67--&amp;gca-ft=197&amp;gca-ds=sophi">football</a>, <a href="https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28913377-rugby-clip/">rugby</a>, and <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2716150/">track, field and cross-country teams</a> have adopted it as far back as 1995.

Spotlight PA could find no instances of the phrase including “protect the innocent” outside of its use by State Police.

A spokesperson for the Pennsylvania State Troopers Association, the union representing troopers, deferred to the department for comment.

Before Monday, the phrase appeared several times throughout the new facilities at the State Police academy in Hershey, which cost <a href="https://www.pennlive.com/news/2024/10/tremendous-progress-inside-the-387m-pa-state-police-academy-construction-project.html">$387.8 million</a> and opened in December 2025.

An image showing the phrase burned into a wooden panel <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Pennsylvania/s/c6Xg6d3zn7">began circulating</a> on social media last week, where users called for accountability and explanation. Commenters took issue with a police organization adopting a phrase more commonly associated with the military.

Spotlight PA has since been able to verify the image was taken inside the tack room, where troopers store saddles, bridles, and other riding equipment.

The burned slogan did not appear in the tack room during an official tour and news conference on Dec, 19, 2025, during which Sgt. Carrie Neidigh showed journalists and staff around the building.

A Spotlight PA review of social media posts turned up other uses of the slogan: <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Pennsylvania/s/SvvW8CAp5i">on a dedication plaque in a seating area</a>, and <a href="https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=1418506053638418&amp;set=pcb.1418506766971680">on a painted wooden plank hung</a> on a wall above the crest of the mounted unit.

The wooden plank appears to predate the new building. Witherite posted a picture of it hanging in the old stables on Facebook in December 2024.

Academy cadets care for the horses as part of their training, said Witherite, who also owns two horses that previously worked with State Police.

“Every single one of us walked under that saying,” he said. “That just went with the mounted unit.”<em></em>
