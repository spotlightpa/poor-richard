HARRISBURG — On Nov. 3, voters across Pennsylvania will head to the polls for the midterm election.

Every voter in every election is entitled to cast their ballot without intimidation, interference, or discrimination.

Voter intimidation is illegal under federal and state law — and according to the Pennsylvania Department of State, it’s also rare. However, several incidents of suspected intimidation in recent years have brought more attention to the issue and prompted action by the state.

During the 2020 election, Pennsylvania officials repeatedly raised concerns that President Donald Trump’s campaign and its supporters <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/22/us/politics/trump-campaign-voter-surveillance.html">might</a> be <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/trump-supporters-convicted-bringing-guns-philadelphia-2020-election-rcna51983">intimidating</a> voters. Since then, Pennsylvania has <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/pages/reportelectioncomplaints.aspx">established a hotline</a> to report cases of voter intimidation and other election-related crimes. It’s also created a <a href="https://www.cityandstatepa.com/policy/2024/02/shapiro-taps-al-schmidt-lead-new-election-threats-task-force/394572/">task force that mitigates election threats and combats misinformation</a>.

Concerns over intimidation have continued this election cycle as the Department of Justice <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/doj-has-monitored-elections-for-decades-heres-why-some-state-officials-are-concerned-this-year">prepares to dispatch 1,000 election monitors</a> to polling places in all 50 states, its largest-ever effort. <a href="https://www.pennlive.com/politics/2026/10/department-of-justice-says-federal-monitors-will-observe-voting-in-four-pa-counties.html">In Pennsylvania</a>, monitors are expected in Allegheny, Lehigh, Luzerne, and Philadelphia Counties.

Justice Department officials have said that monitors will “observe and report,” but voting rights groups, advocates, and state officials have expressed fear of potential <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/doj-monitoring-plans-and-dhs-voter-checks-raise-concerns-of-election-interference">interference and intimidation</a> as Trump and his administration continue to <a href="https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-sows-doubt-mail-voting-encouraging-midterms-approach/story?id=137025278">question the security of elections</a>. A <a href="https://naacp.org/articles/naacp-and-advancement-project-sue-block-doj-plans-deploy-federal-law-enforcement-election">lawsuit was filed</a> in mid-October to block the DOJ plans.

Earlier this year, Trump said he <a href="https://x.com/NewsHour/status/2054282657228279891">had not ruled out</a> sending the National Guard or ICE agents to polling places. However, a Department of Homeland Security spokesperson told Spotlight PA and Votebeat in October that there were no <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/10/midterm-2026-ice-law-enforcement-polls-bill-kenyatta-pennsylvania-elections/">plans for ICE operations</a> there.

<a href="https://pubintlaw.org/know-your-rights-law-enforcement-presence-at-polling-places/">Federal law</a> prohibits federal troops, agents, or law enforcement, <a href="https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/preventing-voter-intimidation-federal-agents-polls">including ICE</a>, from interfering with voting.

Here’s everything you need to know so that you can be prepared if you experience or witness voter intimidation: <strong></strong>

<h2 id="spl-heading-1">What is voter intimidation?</h2>

Voter intimidation can take many forms. It includes but is not limited to explicit physical or verbal threats and interrogation, as well as tactics like photographing or recording voters. This applies inside and outside of a polling place, as well as in county election offices and at mail ballot drop boxes. A more comprehensive list of intimidation tactics covered under the law <a href="https://www.pa.gov/agencies/vote/voter-support/your-rights-and-the-law/voter-intimidation.html">is on the state’s website</a>.

In Pennsylvania, the law also specifies <a href="https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/pennsylvania-protections-against-intimidation-voters-and-election-workers">that it’s illegal</a> to coerce someone to vote or to refrain from voting, to interfere with a person’s ability to vote, or to block the entrance or exit of any polling place.

Election workers can also be subjected to intimidation. In 2022, the FBI and Department of Justice identified Pennsylvania <a href="https://www.axios.com/local/philadelphia/2022/10/19/pennsylvania-election-worker-threats-fbi">as one of the top states</a> for threats to election workers, based on reports made to a special task force.

However, intimidation and misconduct can be performed by anyone, including nonpartisan election workers and partisan poll watchers. The Pennsylvania Department of State has rules on what poll watchers — who are picked by political parties and campaigns — <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2020/10/pa-poll-watchers-can-cant-do-election-day-explainer/">can and cannot do</a>, as well as guidance for poll workers, to ensure that no voter is inappropriately challenged as they cast their ballot or is subjected to any sort of discrimination, intimidation, or misconduct.

Voter intimidation doesn’t exclusively occur at polling places. It can also happen via door-to-door canvassing and include flyers or calls that intentionally disseminate incorrect or false election information, including details about voting time or place, to interfere with people being able to properly cast their ballot.

<h2 id="spl-heading-2">What does the law say?</h2>

<a href="https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/federal-laws-protecting-against-intimidation-voters-and-election-workers">Federal</a> and state protections against intimidation cover the entire voting process, from registration to casting a ballot to counting votes, and apply to both the voter and election workers.

At the federal level, the Voting Rights Act and the Civil Rights Act, in addition to various other laws, make it clear that actual or attempted “intimidation” or “threats” against a person trying to vote are illegal. Committing this sort of behavior during a national election is a federal crime, and a person who intimidates voters during any election can be prosecuted by the Department of Justice.

In Pennsylvania, it is <a href="https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-25-ps-elections-electoral-districts/pa-st-sect-25-3527/">a felony in the third degree</a> to “use or practice any intimidation, threats, force or violence with design to influence unduly or overawe any elector, or to prevent him from voting or restrain his freedom of choice.” Intimidating a voter can result in imprisonment for up to two years.

In addition to that law, the Pennsylvania Department of State has <a href="https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dos/resources/voting-and-elections/directives-and-guidance/2022-09-26-Voter-Intimidation-and-Discriminatory-Conduct.pdf">guidance</a> on voter intimidation and discriminatory conduct, as well as <a href="https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dos/resources/voting-and-elections/directives-and-guidance/2025/2025-04-guidance-rules-atpollingplace-on-electionday-2.1.pdf">rules for polling places</a> on Election Day “to help voters, elections officials, attorneys and watchers understand their respective roles, responsibilities, and rights.”

<h2 id="spl-heading-3">Where’s the line?</h2>

A lot can happen inside and outside of a polling place, and it can be hard to discern what is allowed and what is not. Below are answers to some common questions about behavior at the polls:

<h3 id="spl-heading-4">Are guns allowed in polling places?</h3>

It depends on the polling place. <a href="https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/resources/voting/">Firearms are not allowed in polling places</a> if they are housed in any of the following locations: a private property that forbids firearms, a school, a courthouse, or any other location where Pennsylvania law prohibits the carrying of firearms. This applies even if you are the lawful owner of a firearm.

<h3 id="spl-heading-5">Can I wear clothing in support of the candidate I’m voting for?</h3>

Yes, you can wear clothing, buttons, stickers, and more to show support for the candidate you’re voting for. However, campaigning for the candidate inside the polling place is not allowed and is considered electioneering.

Election officials and poll watchers cannot wear any partisan apparel, including buttons.

<h3 id="spl-heading-6">Is law enforcement allowed in polling places?</h3>

Pennsylvania law generally prohibits police officers from being within 100 feet of a polling place during voting hours. However, some exceptions exist, including for officers who are there to vote or have been called to respond to a disturbance.

<h2 id="spl-heading-7">What should I do if I experience voter intimidation or witness it?</h2>

If you are a victim of intimidation, or witness it, you should report it to the district attorney and your county election office, either <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/ReportElectionComplaints.aspx">online</a> or by finding the phone number for your <a href="https://www.pa.gov/agencies/vote/contact-us/contact-your-election-officials.html">county board of elections</a>.

You can also report any instances of voter intimidation, election fraud, or other election crimes to the Pennsylvania Department of State&#39;s voter hotline at 1-877-VOTESPA (1-877-868-3772) or file an election complaint <a href="https://www.pa.gov/services/vote/file-an-election-complaint">online</a>.

Once complaints are received, the Office of Attorney General can investigate and prosecute voter intimidation.

If you have an immediate concern for your safety or the safety of others, you should call 911.
