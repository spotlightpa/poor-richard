The Clairton Coke Works was built in 1901.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The accident “should never have happened,” said CSB Chairperson Steve Owens in a &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.csb.gov/us-chemical-safety-board-issues-final-report-on-august-2025-fatal-coke-oven-gas-explosion-at-us-steel-clairton-coke-works/&#34;&gt;press release&lt;/a&gt; about the report.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The board—whose conclusions are only advisory and do not require the company to act on them—also accused U.S. Steel of failing to develop a formal protocol for washing valves despite relying on the process during maintenance outages. MPW did not respond to a request for comment.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;“The CSB determined the cause of the incident was the over-pressurization of a double-disc gate valve constructed of cast iron, which resulted in the release of flammable coke-oven gas that ignited and exploded,” the agency said in the 85-page report.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The board is charged with investigating, determining and reporting to the public the facts and causes of any accidental chemical release that kills or seriously injures people or causes substantial property damage.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;In February, the federal Occupational Safety and Health Administration&lt;a href=&#34;https://www.osha.gov/ords/imis/establishment.inspection_detail?id=1843132.015&#34;&gt; cited&lt;/a&gt; U.S. Steel with nine “serious” violations and one non-serious violation in connection with the 2025 incident, and fined the company $118,214. The company appealed those violations, and the case awaits a hearing in federal court. U.S. Steel said it does not comment on what it called an “active administrative litigation.”&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;In June 2025, U.S. Steel became a &lt;a href=&#34;https://insideclimatenews.org/news/30052025/trump-celebrates-japan-nippon-us-steel-deal-pennsylvania-pollution/&#34;&gt;wholly owned subsidiary&lt;/a&gt; of Japan’s Nippon Steel Corp. which paid $14.9 billion for the American industrial icon.
