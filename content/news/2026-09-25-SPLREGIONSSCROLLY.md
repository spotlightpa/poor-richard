{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Central PA" color="Blue" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzwt/central-pa-a-edited.geojson" image="2026/05/01n1-dy4v-yvs2-mjba.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Central Pennsylvania" image-caption="Farmland stretches toward the ridges of Central Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
Ask where Central PA begins and you'll start an argument. Most versions are anchored by State College and the Susquehanna Valley, but the edges drift depending on whether you grew up closer to Altoona or Harrisburg.

This map is one answer: the ridges and valleys that people usually mean when they say "the middle of the state."
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Coal Region" color="Orange" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzx4/coal-region-a-edited.geojson" image="2025/04/01m0-t8sg-qd9a-zpg0.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Pennsylvania's Coal Region" image-caption="Rowhomes line a hillside in a Coal Region town." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
The Coal Region is defined less by geography than by history. It follows the anthracite fields that powered the state's industrial boom, through towns like Pottsville, Shamokin, and Hazleton.

For many people who grew up there, the name is part of their identity, even decades after most of the mines closed.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Eastern PA" color="Teal" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzx9/eastern-pa-a-edited.geojson" image="2026/04/01mw-x5py-70yr-mcez.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Eastern Pennsylvania" image-caption="A river valley in Eastern Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
Eastern PA is the broadest label on this list, and the fuzziest. For some people it means everything east of the Susquehanna. For others it stops well short of Philadelphia's orbit.

This version draws the line wide, taking in the Poconos, the Lehigh Valley, and the southeast.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Lehigh Valley" color="Green" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzxd/lehigh-valley-a.geojson" fit="shaded">}}
The Lehigh Valley is the rare region with a clear answer. Allentown, Bethlehem, and Easton anchor it, and Lehigh and Northampton counties are the core almost everyone agrees on.

The debate is mostly about the edges, and whether neighboring counties belong in the Valley or just near it.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="North-central PA" color="Navy" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzxk/north-central-pa-a-edited.geojson" image="2023/10/01jk-jbv6-dvzc-5m0w.jpeg" image-description="A scene in North-central Pennsylvania" image-caption="Forested hills in north-central Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
North-central PA is the state's most rural stretch: the Pennsylvania Wilds, the Pine Creek Gorge, and some of the darkest night skies on the East Coast.

It's also where the lines get blurry, since towns here are often closer to New York than to Harrisburg.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Northeastern PA" color="Red" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzxr/nepa-a-edited.geojson" image="2026/04/01my-hxqc-1z2v-7p39.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Northeastern Pennsylvania" image-caption="A downtown street in Northeastern Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
To most people, Northeastern PA means Scranton, Wilkes-Barre, and the valleys around them. Locals shorten it to NEPA.

The question is how far out it goes: north toward the New York border, east into the Poconos, or south into Coal Region country.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Northwestern PA" color="Blue" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzy1/nwpa-a-edited.geojson" image="2025/03/01ky-dpjw-jfp7-yjt4.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Northwestern Pennsylvania" image-caption="The shoreline of Lake Erie in Northwestern Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
Northwestern PA is built around Erie and the shoreline of Lake Erie, the state's only Great Lakes coast.

Inland, it shades into forests and small towns, and the line between the northwest and the Pittsburgh area is a common point of disagreement.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Philadelphia area" color="Orange" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzy5/philly-area-a.geojson" fit="shaded">}}
The Philadelphia area starts with the city itself, but few people stop there. The suburban counties are so tied to Philadelphia by commutes, sports, and media that many residents claim the region as their own.

This map zooms in on the core of that orbit.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Pittsburgh area" color="Yellow" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzye/pittsburgh-area-a-edited.geojson" fit="shaded">}}
Like Philadelphia, the Pittsburgh area reaches well past the city limits. Allegheny County is the center, but its neighbors share the same rivers, TV stations, and accent.

Where the Pittsburgh area ends and Southwest PA begins is one of the harder calls on this list.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="South-central PA" color="Teal" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzyk/south-central-pa-a-edited.geojson" image="2024/02/01jx-z1g9-fr55-mgm8.jpeg" image-description="A scene in South-central Pennsylvania" image-caption="Farm fields in South-central Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
South-central PA runs along the Maryland line, from Gettysburg and York to Lancaster and Harrisburg. It's farm country and state government, Amish buggies and warehouse corridors.

People here often split on whether they're "central" or "south-central," and this map takes one side of that debate.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Southeastern PA" color="Green" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzys/sepa-a-edited.geojson" image="2024/06/01k6-rs2g-33jq-6eqr.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Southeastern Pennsylvania" image-caption="A small-town main street in Southeastern Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
Southeastern PA overlaps heavily with the Philadelphia area, but the label tends to stretch farther west and north, into Chester County horse country and the edges of Pennsylvania Dutch Country.

It's the state's most populous corner, and its boundaries depend on who's drawing them.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Southwest PA" color="Navy" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzyy/southwest-pa-a-edited.geojson">}}
Southwest PA includes Pittsburgh's orbit and then keeps going, into the hills of Washington, Greene, and Fayette counties along the West Virginia line.

It's coal and gas country with deep steel-town roots, and many residents see it as distinct from the city itself.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
{{<featured/scrolly-map-stop label="Western PA" color="Red" geojson="https://files.data.spotlightpa.org/uploads/01nb/mzz2/western-pa-a-edited.geojson" image="2025/01/01ks-qgb8-j8x7-k8zm.jpeg" image-description="A scene in Western Pennsylvania" image-caption="A river town in Western Pennsylvania." image-credit="Photographer Name / Spotlight PA">}}
Western PA is the widest net of all. For a lot of people it simply means everything that roots for the Steelers instead of the Eagles.

This version takes that idea seriously, stretching from Erie to the Mason-Dixon Line.
{{</featured/scrolly-map-stop>}}
