Sign up for their newsletter &lt;a href=&#34;https://insideclimatenews.org/newsletter/&#34;&gt;here&lt;/a&gt;.&lt;/i&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Energy Transfer, the Dallas-based pipeline giant facing a state-level criminal probe related to &lt;a href=&#34;https://insideclimatenews.org/news/11082026/pennsylvania-neighborhood-deals-with-fuel-spill-as-environmental-enforcement-shrinks/&#34;&gt;a jet fuel spill &lt;/a&gt;that polluted drinking water in a suburban Philadelphia neighborhood, is seeking to have part of Pennsylvania’s 89-year-old Clean Streams Law declared unconstitutional.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The extraordinary &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28601594-198-md-2026-4142026-petition-for-review/&#34;&gt;lawsuit,&lt;/a&gt; filed quietly in April, asks Pennsylvania’s Commonwealth Court to declare one or more sections of the law unconstitutionally vague under the due process clauses of both the state constitution and the U.S. Constitution.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Energy Transfer’s objection to the law: Parties could be found guilty of an environmental crime even if their actions are accidental. Because one section of the law lacks what is called a “&lt;em&gt;mens rea&lt;/em&gt;,” or “guilty mind” provision, the company said, parties could face fines or imprisonment whether or not their actions were knowing and purposeful.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;“A lack of &lt;em&gt;mens rea &lt;/em&gt;requirement here offends the principles of justice and does not provide adequate notice of the offending conduct that could form the basis for criminal liability,” Energy Transfer’s petition said.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;But the challenge comes before any charges have been filed against Energy Transfer. in a sprawling criminal case over industrial waste spills and drinking water pollution at 22 sites across 11 Pennsylvania counties. The plea covered incidents that occurred during construction of the company’s cross-state Mariner East II natural gas products pipeline and an explosion on its Revolution gas pipeline that leveled a home and a barn in western Pennsylvania. &lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;That case, brought by Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro when he was state attorney general, was partly about the Clean Streams Law but also cited other laws and violations, including failure to report contamination to authorities.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The probe Energy Transfer now faces could be similarly complex. The company is under fire not only because of the jet fuel leak found early last year on the Twin Oaks pipeline. It also faces a slew of lawsuits that focus on its failure to detect the underground spill despite resident complaints of a gasoline smell and taste in their drinking water for 16 months prior to the January 2025 discovery. The company also did not notify the federal Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration of the repeated complaints, the agency &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28497489-phmsa-nopso-sunoco-energy-transfer-42025054-02132025-0/&#34;&gt;has said. &lt;/a&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;By the time the pipeline breach was discovered, six homes in Upper Makefield Township, Bucks County, had jet fuel in their drinking water wells.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Since then, toxic chemicals like benzene and xylenes, components of fuel, have been detected in the wells of at least 28 homes and the company has paid for home treatment systems in more than 200 homes to protect against contamination. The company has recently proposed a cleanup plan, involving vacuum extraction and long-term water testing, that could take years.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;At a public community meeting soon after the spill was discovered, a representative of the Bucks County district attorney’s office said it had asked the attorney general’s office to investigate the spill. A spokesperson for the attorney general’s office &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.inquirer.com/business/energy/sunoco-pipeline-spill-bucks-county-20250317.html&#34;&gt;confirmed to The Philadelphia Inquirer&lt;em&gt; &lt;/em&gt;&lt;/a&gt;that it was investigating in March 2025. Energy Transfer disclosed the investigation to investors in &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28506095-energy-transfer-10k-2026/#document/p101/a2824239&#34;&gt;its annual report&lt;/a&gt; in February 2026: “Potential charges, penalties or damages are not known at this time,” the company said.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;But two months later, the company filed its challenge of the Pennsylvania Clean Streams law in Commonwealth Court, the court with jurisdiction over cases against the state. Under that section, &lt;a href=&#34;https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?SESSYR=1937&amp;SESSIND=0&amp;ACTNUM=394&amp;SMTHLWIND=&amp;CHPT=6&amp;SCTN=2&amp;SUBSCTN=&#34;&gt;602 (a) of the Clean Streams Law&lt;/a&gt;, any person could face a fine of $100 to $10,000 for pollution discharges into “waters of the Commonwealth” and could face a 90-day prison sentence for failure to pay the fine.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;That section does not discuss state of mind, but other sections of the law do, giving prosecutors other options for charging violators who engaged in either purposeful or reckless acts. There are higher fines and greater prison sentences in the law for either negligent behavior (misdemeanors) or knowing and intentional actions (felonies).&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;In his briefs, Sunday said his office had taken no position on how the Clean Streams Law applied to the case, and he raised the possibility that the criminal investigation may not be about the water pollution at all. They didn’t say that they’re going to use this statute at all.”&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Sarachan spent many years handling white collar crime cases in the U.S. Attorney’s Office in Philadelphia, working often with Pennsylvania prosecutors, and so is familiar with the range of options they have under the state’s Clean Streams Law. “There’s really a tremendous amount of doubt that they would ever be resorting to the provision that this lawsuit is trying to have declared unconstitutional,” Sarachan said.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;h2 class=&#34;wp-block-heading&#34;&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Upending Decades of Well-Settled Law?&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h2&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;But Energy Transfer’s lawsuit could have a far broader impact than on one provision of the Clean Streams Law.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;The suit argues that part of Pennsylvania’s Criminal Code also is unconstitutional because it has allowed the state legislature to pass laws without a &lt;em&gt;mens rea&lt;/em&gt; for summary offenses. Khan’s office did not respond to a request for comment.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Sunday also successfully had the case sealed from public view temporarily in August, arguing that to respond to Energy Transfer’s lawsuit, it would be forced to disclose information “expressly made confidential under Pennsylvania law.” Although Sunday’s briefs did not specify the reason for the confidentiality, prosecutors typically hold the details of a criminal investigation in confidence unless and until they are prepared to file charges.&lt;/p&gt;&#10;&#10;&#10;&#10;&lt;p class=&#34;wp-block-paragraph&#34;&gt;Energy Transfer objected to Sunday’s request and after a closed-to-the-public oral argument, Commonwealth Court Judge Matthew Wolf unsealed the majority of the case filings on Aug. 19.
