HARRISBURG — Pennsylvania’s 2026 general election is quickly approaching. If you still have questions about where your polling place is, who is on the ballot, and how to vote, Spotlight PA has you covered.

On Tuesday, Nov. 3, voters across the commonwealth will cast their ballots for governor and lieutenant governor, along with the entire Pennsylvania House and half of the state Senate. All of Pennsylvania’s 17 congressional districts will also be up for election. There may be local ballot questions, depending where you live.

Before you submit your ballot, here’s a last-minute checklist of everything you’ll need to vote.

First, the basics:

- Make sure you’re registered. If you aren’t, it is unfortunately too late to register to vote in the upcoming election — the deadline was Oct. 19. You can check your voter registration status <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/pages/voterregistrationstatus.aspx">on the Pennsylvania Department of State website</a>.

- The deadline to <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/OnlineAbsenteeApplication/#/OnlineMailInBegin">request a mail or absentee ballot</a> is Oct. 27. Your <a href="https://www.pa.gov/agencies/vote/contact-us/contact-your-election-officials">county election office</a> has to receive your application by 5 p.m. that day. If you have already applied for a mail ballot, you can track it on the <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/BallotTracking.aspx">Pennsylvania Department of State&#39;s election ballot status webpage</a>. To be counted, your mail ballot must be received by your county election office by 8 p.m. on Election Day. <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/10/pennsylvania-general-election-2026-mail-ballot-how-to-request-fill-out-return-elections/">Read more about mail ballots and how to properly fill them out</a>.

- If you plan to vote in person, you can find your polling place <a href="https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/PollingPlaceInfo.aspx">online</a>.

- You need to bring a form of ID such as a driver’s license or a passport if it’s your first time voting at your polling location. You can find the full list of acceptable IDs <a href="https://www.pa.gov/agencies/vote/voter-support/new-voters.html">on Pennsylvania&#39;s official election website</a>.

- Whether you’re voting in person or by mail, here’s <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/10/pennsylvania-general-election-day-2026-voter-rights-elections/">everything you need to know</a> about your Election Day voting rights.

- For general voting and election resources, check out Spotlight PA’s <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/elections/">2026 Election Center</a>.

- Still have questions? You can reach out to your county election office for answers. Find contact information for your county on the <a href="https://www.vote.pa.gov/Resources/Pages/Contact-Your-Election-Officials.aspx">Pennsylvania election website</a>.

Here’s what you can expect to see on your ballot:

- In <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/09/josh-shapiro-stacy-garrity-pennsylvania-governor-election-guide-elections/#spl-heading-4">the race for governor</a>, voters will choose between Democratic incumbent Josh Shapiro, Republican state treasurer Stacy Garrity, and Libertarian Ken Krawchuk. Each gubernatorial candidate is running alongside <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/04/pennsylvania-primary-election-lieutenant-governor-guide-davis-richey-ventre-elections/">a candidate for lieutenant governor</a>. <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/10/pennsylvania-election-governor-candidates-quiz/">Take our quiz</a> to see which major party candidate for governor most closely aligns with what matters to you, and <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/04/pennsylvania-election-governor-josh-shapiro-stacy-garrity-campaign-finance-visualized-elections/">use this tool</a> to see how much money Shapiro and Garrity have raised for their campaigns.

- Aside from statewide races, the races and candidates on your ballot will depend on where you live. You may be able to find a sample ballot on your official county website, which you can locate using the <a href="https://www.pa.gov/agencies/vote/contact-us/contact-your-election-officials">Pennsylvania Department of State voting and election webpage</a>.

- All of Pennsylvania’s 17 congressional districts are on the ballot this year. Use <a href="https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative">this tool</a> to find your district and <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/09/pennsylvania-2026-congressional-election-guide-tossup-districts-elections/">check out our guide</a> that covers all of the races, including the three that could determine which party controls the U.S. House for the next two years.

- The entire Pennsylvania House and half the state Senate are on the ballot this year. Learn <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/09/races-to-watch-pennsylvania-house-senate-2026-midterm-election-elections/">which races</a> could determine control of the Pennsylvania General Assembly and check out Spotlight PA’s <a href="https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/10/pennsylvania-general-election-2026-legislative-house-senate-races-candidate-vetting-guide-elections/">guide to vetting candidates</a> to help you evaluate which candidates you want to send to or keep in Harrisburg.

There are no statewide ballot measures in November, but some voters may see local questions — for instance, <a href="https://www.lwvpgh.org/ballot">Allegheny County</a> and <a href="https://thephiladelphiacitizen.org/ballot-questions/">Philadelphia</a> both have them. Check your <a href="https://www.vote.pa.gov/Resources/Pages/Contact-Your-Election-Officials.aspx">county’s election website</a> or sample ballot to learn exactly which races, candidates, and questions you will be voting for.
