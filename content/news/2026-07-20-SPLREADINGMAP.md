Reading&#39;s city council is organized into five districts, each represented by a council member elected to serve residents in that specific area.

{{</featured/dropcap>}}

Understanding which district you live in is the first step toward knowing who represents you at City Hall. Use the interactive map above to find your district by entering your address, or by clicking directly on the map to see district boundaries.

Council meetings are typically open to the public and provide a chance for residents to weigh in on issues before votes are taken. Meeting schedules, agendas, and minutes are usually posted on the city&#39;s official website in advance.

Each district covers a distinct part of Reading, and boundaries can shift over time as the city&#39;s population changes. If you&#39;ve recently moved, it&#39;s worth checking the map again even if you already knew your district before.

Beyond council members, understanding your district can also help you connect with neighbors, track local development proposals, and follow spending decisions that affect your specific area of the city.

The city&#39;s five council districts each elect one representative to serve alongside the council president, who is elected citywide. Together, they make up Reading&#39;s legislative body, responsible for passing ordinances, approving the annual budget, and overseeing city departments.

{{<featured/keypoints>}}
### Key Points

- Reading City Council has five district seats plus at-large representation.
- Council members serve four-year terms and vote on budgets, zoning, and local ordinances.
- Residents can find their council member's contact information through the city's website once they know their district.

{{</featured/keypoints>}}

Council members also serve on committees that focus on specific issues, such as public safety, finance, or community development. Residents interested in a particular issue can often find out which committee handles it and attend those meetings directly.

Redistricting typically follows the U.S. Census every ten years, meaning district lines you&#39;re familiar with today could shift in the future. Staying informed about redistricting proposals is one way residents can weigh in before new maps are finalized.

In addition to council members, Reading residents are represented by a mayor elected citywide, along with state and federal representatives whose districts overlap differently with city council boundaries. Knowing your council district doesn&#39;t automatically tell you your state or federal districts, those are worth checking separately.

Local elections for city council typically draw lower turnout than state or federal races, even though council decisions often have a more direct, day-to-day impact on residents&#39; lives, from road repairs to zoning changes in their own neighborhood.

If the map doesn&#39;t return a result for your address, try entering just the street name and number without an apartment or unit designation, or contact the city clerk&#39;s office directly for help identifying your district.
